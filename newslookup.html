The trader's problem

By the time you've read it, the move is over.

Markets react to news in minutes. But the news is scattered across business portals, exchange and regulator notices, and a flood of general stories. Nobody can read all of it — so something important always slips past.

Too many places to look

Economic Times, Moneycontrol, Mint, Business Standard, SEBI and RBI notices — every tab is another chance to miss a story.

Too much noise

Personal-finance columns, festival features and lifestyle stories sit between the headlines that actually matter.

Too little time

You need to know in seconds whether the news is turning bullish or bearish — not after reading two hundred articles.

One screen

Every source in one live feed, every headline already scored.

NewsLookup collects all the sources you follow into a single newest-first list. Each headline carries its own mood badge and a confidence percentage, and strong stories get a green or red edge — so a quick scroll tells you what deserves your attention.

  • Positive · 95% Negative · 97% on every headline, with the source and how long ago it landed.
  • Ready-made catalog: Economic Times, Moneycontrol, Business Standard, Mint, The Hindu BusinessLine and Google News — plus SEBI and RBI notices straight from the regulators.
  • Any RSS or Atom feed can be added by URL and tagged by country.
  • Follow Indian and US markets together: the overall mood is shown separately for each country, so one market never hides the other.
  • Click a headline to open the full story in your browser.

Search Google News for any stock, company or theme.

You don't need a source in the catalog. Type Reliance, HDFC Bank or Crude oil and NewsLookup turns it into its own live Google News search — every headline scored, with a mood of its own, so your position never dissolves into the crowd.

  • Always fresh: each search looks at the last 24 hours and refreshes every 30 minutes, so you read today's news, not an archive.
  • Same scoring, same filter: keyword headlines are scored on your PC and pass through the same market-news filter as every other source.

Signal, not noise

It sets aside what isn't market news, so the score means something.

General news feeds are full of stories that have nothing to do with your trades. NewsLookup checks every headline and labels it Market or Off-topic. Off-topic items stay visible but dimmed, and they don't count toward the sentiment score.

  • Built for Indian and US markets: it recognises Sensex, Nifty, F&O, block deals, upper circuits, FII/DII flows, repo rate, IPOs, earnings, Fed and more.
  • Market sections and regulator feeds (SEBI, RBI) count in full — so a quarterly result or "Gold rises to a one-week high" always counts.
  • The screen says exactly what was left out: "43 off-topic items excluded."
  • Disagree with a label? One click flips it, and NewsLookup remembers your choice.
  • Flip on Market only to hide everything else in the list and the chart.
  • Delete a single junk story without unsubscribing from the whole feed.

You decide what matters

Star the sources that count — they carry triple weight.

A busy feed can shout down a quiet one that matters more. NewsLookup blends your sources fairly: each source gets one vote, however many stories it publishes, and any source you star counts three times. Give the regulators and your best market desk more say, and the overall mood follows.

  • Every source has a mood dot — green, red or grey — so you see which way each one is leaning at a glance.
  • Star SEBI, RBI or your favourite desk and they pull the blended score harder.
  • A source that fails to load is flagged "fetch failed", so you know when you're not seeing the full picture.
  • Freshness is always visible: "updated 6m ago" under every source.

System requirements

  • Windows: Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, with the Microsoft WebView2 runtime (already included in current Windows updates).
  • Linux: 64-bit distribution with GTK 3 and WebKitGTK installed.
  • Disk space: about 150 MB free — the app plus the one-time sentiment model download (about 110 MB).
  • Internet: needed on first run to download the model, and to fetch fresh headlines from your sources.

Up and running in four steps

  1. Install NewsLookup.
  2. On first launch, tick your news sources and add keywords for the stocks you follow.
  3. Let the sentiment model finish its one-time download.
  4. Watch your headlines fill in, scored and sorted — then keep it open beside your trading screen.
